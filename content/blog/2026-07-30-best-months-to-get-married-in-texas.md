## What Are the Best Months to Get Married in Texas Weather?

Here's the ranking I give every bride who asks me, based on what I've actually seen hold up on wedding days across the DFW area.

**Tier 1 — Late October and November.** This is the window. By mid-to-late October the humidity has genuinely broken, average highs sit in the 70s, and by November you're often in the 60s. Evening ceremonies get that low golden light photographers plan their whole timeline around. Hair holds. Makeup doesn't move. If you have flexibility on your date, start here.

**Tier 2 — April and early May.** Spring is beautiful and comfortable, with highs in the 70s and 80s, and the wildflowers are doing their thing. The tradeoff is storms. Texas spring is our most volatile severe-weather stretch, and an outdoor spring wedding needs a genuine rain plan, not a vague hope. Comfort-wise it's excellent. Certainty-wise it isn't.

**Tier 3 — March, early June, and December.** Workable with planning. March can swing thirty degrees in a week. Early June is warm but not yet brutal. December is cool and often lovely, though you're negotiating with holiday schedules and shorter days — a 4:00 p.m. ceremony in December is already losing light.

**Tier 4 — Late June through early September.** I'll be direct: this is the hard season. Afternoon highs regularly sit in the upper 90s and push past 100, and the humidity in early September is still very much summer humidity. Beautiful weddings happen in these months constantly — but they happen indoors, or they happen at 7:00 p.m., or they happen with a genuinely serious plan.

You can check the long-term climate normals for the DFW area yourself through the [National Weather Service Fort Worth office](https://www.weather.gov/fwd/) — it's the same data your venue coordinator is quietly looking at. The [NOAA climate averages](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/us-climate-normals/) for the region tell the same story.

## The Fall Trap Nobody Warns You About

Here is the single most common mistake I see, and it costs brides more comfort than any other date decision:

**Early fall in Texas is not fall. It's summer wearing a cardigan it hasn't put on yet.**

A September 14th wedding sounds like a fall wedding. You've pinned burnt-orange bouquets and a moody terracotta palette. Then the day arrives and it's 94 degrees with the kind of humidity that makes the air feel like a held breath. Your bridesmaids are in long sleeves because the invitation said "autumn." Your veil is sticking to your shoulders.

I've worked that wedding. More than once.

The temperature in North Texas doesn't reliably turn until somewhere around the second or third week of October. Before that, you are planning a summer wedding with fall decor. That's a completely valid choice — just make it on purpose, and dress your wedding party for the actual forecast rather than the aesthetic.

If your heart is set on genuine sweater weather and golden light without a heat contingency, **the last two weeks of October and all of November are where you want to be.**

## Humidity-Proof Bridal Hairstyles for Outdoor Weddings

Whatever month you land on, if any part of your day is outdoors in Texas, humidity is a factor. Here's what actually survives it.

**Structured beats loose — every time.** A soft, undone wave is the most-requested look I get, and it is also the most fragile thing you can ask a Texas afternoon to preserve. Humidity swells the hair shaft, the curl relaxes, and by cocktail hour you've got a shape that's flatter and wider than what you saw in the mirror. If you want that romantic softness, ask your stylist for a look that's *built* to fall — a style pinned with structure underneath that reads soft on the surface. The support is invisible. The staying power isn't.

**Updos hold. Half-ups negotiate. Down styles are a gamble.** That's the honest hierarchy. An updo removes the variable entirely — the hair is secured, the shape is set, and humidity has very little to work with. A half-up gives you movement while keeping the crown controlled. A fully down style in August is asking a lot.

**Texture is your ally, not your enemy.** Naturally curly and coily hair often does *better* in Texas humidity than fine straight hair fighting to hold a curl, because you're working with the hair's natural behavior instead of against it. If you have texture, consider a look that celebrates it rather than one that flat-irons it into something the weather will undo by 6:00 p.m.

**Ask about the products, specifically.** A flexible-hold spray layered through the build holds better in humidity than one heavy shell of hairspray at the end, which can crack and separate. This is worth asking your stylist about directly at your [bridal hair and makeup trial](/blog/2026-06-17-what-to-expect-at-a-bridal-hair-and-makeup-trial) — and worth testing by walking outside during that appointment.

## How to Make Wedding Makeup Last All Day at an Outdoor Ceremony

Makeup longevity in Texas is a preparation problem far more than a product problem. What happens before the foundation goes on matters more than what's in the bottle.

**Skin prep is most of the job.** Hydrated, balanced, properly primed skin holds makeup dramatically better than skin that's been over-exfoliated the week of the wedding or slathered in something new the night before. Whatever your routine is, do not change it in the final two weeks. A "brightening" mask three days out is how brides end up with reactive skin in their photos.

**Long-wear formulas, applied thin.** Thin, buildable layers cling. Thick single layers slide — especially anywhere you sweat. This is one of the biggest differences between a look that reads fresh at 9:00 p.m. and one that's broken up by the reception.

**Setting is not one step.** Powder where you actually need it — through the T-zone, under the eyes, anywhere there's movement — and leave the rest of the face alone so it still looks like skin. A setting spray at the end locks the layers together.

**Waterproof anything near your eyes.** You're going to cry. Your mother is going to cry. Somebody is going to give a toast that gets everyone. Waterproof mascara and a smudge-proof liner aren't precautions, they're just accurate planning.

**Blotting papers, not powder, for touch-ups.** Powder on top of a warm face all day builds into something heavy and gray in photos. Blot the oil off first, then add the smallest amount of powder — or often none at all.

**And the thing that matters most:** build in a shaded, air-conditioned pocket before photos. Twenty minutes indoors before you step out for portraits does more for how your look photographs than any product I could put on your face. If your timeline doesn't have that gap, ask your planner to make one. It's the single highest-value change you can make to an outdoor Texas timeline.

## Why Fall Suits the Texas Boho Aesthetic So Well

There's a reason the warm, earthy, slightly undone look has taken over North Texas weddings — and fall is when it actually works.

The palette that defines it (terracotta, dusty rose, sage, cream, warm neutrals) is built from the same colors the landscape turns in late October. A bouquet of dried grasses and muted blooms looks styled in November and looks slightly out of season in April. The light does the rest: that low, warm, late-afternoon fall sun is genuinely different from summer's overhead glare, and it's flattering in a way you don't have to work for.

For hair and makeup, that means a fall Texas wedding lets me lean into warmth honestly — a soft bronzed skin finish, a lip in the terracotta-to-rose family, hair with visible texture and movement rather than a glassy set. It's a look that belongs to the season instead of fighting it.

If you're planning around one of the lake venues out toward [Heath and Rockwall](/locations/heath), late fall is also when the water and the tree line do you the most favors in photographs.

## Book Earlier Than You Think — Especially for Fall

Here's the practical consequence of everything above: **everyone else has also figured out that October and November are the best months.**

Peak fall Saturdays in the DFW market are the first dates to go, across every vendor category. Photographers, venues, planners, and yes, hair and makeup artists. I regularly have brides reach out in June for an October date that same year and I'm already booked.

If a fall wedding is what you want:

- **Lock the venue and the date first**, then start on vendors immediately.
- **Book your beauty team nine to twelve months out** for a peak fall Saturday. That's not a scarcity pitch, it's just the calendar.
- **Reserve your trial date at the same time as your wedding date**, even though the trial itself should happen two to three months before the wedding.
- **Ask about party size early.** A bride plus six bridesmaids on a Saturday morning is a different staffing conversation than a bride alone, and it needs to happen before the date is tight. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best month to get married in Texas?**  
November, with late October a very close second. Average highs sit in the 60s and 70s, humidity has broken, and the late-afternoon light is at its best for photography. It's also the most competitive month for booking vendors, so plan further ahead than you think you need to.

**What is the worst month for a wedding in Texas?**  
Late July and August. Afternoon highs regularly reach or exceed 100°F, and there's no realistic outdoor ceremony window before evening. If you're marrying in these months, plan an indoor ceremony or a start time after sunset, and build air conditioning into every part of the timeline.

**Is September a good month for a Texas wedding?**  
Early September is still summer in practice — expect heat in the 90s and real humidity. Late September starts to soften. If you want a genuine fall feel with fall weather, aim for the last two weeks of October or November instead.

**How do I keep my wedding makeup from sweating off outdoors?**  
Focus on prep and timeline more than products. Keep your skincare routine unchanged for two weeks before the wedding, use long-wear formulas in thin layers, set strategically rather than all over, and build a shaded or air-conditioned break into the schedule before photos. Blotting papers beat powder for touch-ups.

**What bridal hairstyle holds up best in Texas humidity?**  
An updo, or a style with hidden structure underneath that reads soft on the surface. Fully down, loose styles are the most likely to lose their shape by cocktail hour. If you have natural texture, a look that works with it will almost always outlast one that fights it.

**How far in advance should I book hair and makeup for a fall wedding?**  
Nine to twelve months for a peak-season Saturday in October or November. Those dates fill first across every vendor category. Book your wedding date and your trial date together, and schedule the trial itself for two to three months before the wedding.
